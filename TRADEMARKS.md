# Trademark Policy

## Introduction

This document, the "Policy", describes how the trademarks of
Cocreiamo di Bilotta Francesco ("Cocreiamo", "we") may be used. Our
software is available under the GNU Affero General Public License,
version 3, but the copyright license does not include an implied right
or license to use our trademarks.

Trademarks tell people who stands behind a product or service. An open
source license lets anyone modify the software, so we cannot be sure that
a modified version will not mislead people if it is distributed under the
same name. This Policy describes when you may use our trademarks.

We are not trying to limit lawful use of our trademarks. We describe what
we consider lawful use to be, so that you can understand whether we will
consider your use licensed or non-infringing. If you want to use our
trademarks in a way this Policy does not describe, see
[Where to get further information](#where-to-get-further-information).
Any use that does not comply with this Policy, and that we have not
separately permitted in writing, is not a use we have approved; you must
decide for yourself whether it is nevertheless lawful.

## Our commitment to open source principles

We want to encourage the community to use our trademarks, in a way that
keeps them meaningful as an indicator of the source and quality of our
software and services. This Policy balances two needs: that our trademarks
remain reliable indicators of what they stand for, and that community
members can participate fully in the project.

The copyright license is not affected by this Policy. You may run, study,
modify, host and redistribute the software as the GNU Affero General Public
License allows. This Policy only governs the names and logos you use when
you do so.

## Universal considerations for all uses

Whenever you use one of the Marks, you must do so in a way that does not
mislead anyone, directly or by omission, about exactly what they are getting
and from whom. For example, you may not say you are distributing or hosting
Anima software when it is a modified version, because people would not get
the features and functionality they would get from us. You may not use our
Logos in a way that suggests your website is official or that we endorse it.
You may say that you like Anima software, that you participate in the
community, that you provide an unmodified version, or that you wrote a book
about it.

This requirement, that it is always clear what people are getting and from
whom, runs through this whole Policy. Use it as your guide when you are not
sure.

In addition:

- You may not use the Marks in association with the use or distribution of
  the software if you do not comply with its copyright license.
- You may not use or register, in whole or in part, the Marks as part of
  your own trademark, service mark, domain name, company name, trade name,
  product name or service name.
- You may not use an obvious variation of the Marks, or any phonetic
  equivalent, foreign language equivalent, takeoff or abbreviation, for a
  similar or compatible product or service. We would consider the following
  too similar to our Marks:
  - any mark containing the letters COCREIAMO;
  - any mark combining ANIMA with a term for software, applications, hosting
    or business management (for example "AnimaERP" or "Anima Cloud").
- You will not acquire any rights in the Marks, and any goodwill generated by
  your use of the Marks benefits us alone.

When you redistribute an unmodified copy of an official release, you are not

#### Distribution of modified software

You may use the Word Marks, but not the Logos, to truthfully describe the

modifications, you are the origin of that version: put your own name on it,

#### Statements about compatibility, interoperability or derivation

You may use the Word Marks, but not the Logos, to truthfully describe the
relationship between your software or service and ours. Put our Mark after a
verb or preposition that describes the relationship. You may say "Bob's
plugin for the Anima platform", but not "Bob's Anima plugin". Other examples:

- [your product] works with Anima software
- [your product] is compatible with Cocreiamo
- [your product] is a plugin for Anima software
- [your service] runs on Anima software
- [your service] is powered by Anima software

Configuration, data, plugins and extensions you write for the software are
yours, and you may describe them in this way.

#### Hosting and services

You may host the software and offer services around it, such as hosting,
consulting, development, training and support, as the copyright license
allows. You may describe them truthfully in the ways listed above, for
example "Example Ltd, consulting for Anima software". Using the Marks in the

## General considerations about trademarks

### What is a trademark?

A trademark is a word, phrase, symbol or design, or a combination of them,
that identifies and distinguishes the source of the goods of one party from
those of others. A service mark does the same for a service. "Trade dress"
is the look and feel of the packaging, which here includes the layout,
colours, images and design of a web page. In this Policy, "trademark" and
"mark" cover trademarks, service marks and trade dress.

Using a word functionally inside the software, for example in a file,
folder, directory, path, package or identifier name, is not use as a
trademark and is not an infringement.

### What is "likelihood of confusion"?

A use infringes a trademark when it is likely to confuse or deceive people
about the source of a product or service. If a third party offers training
called "Anima Certification", people would likely believe, wrongly, that we
offer it.

### What is "nominative" use?

Trademark law allows the use of someone else's trademark where it is needed
to identify their product, for example to state the intended purpose of a
product or service. A car repair shop must be allowed to say that it repairs
Volkswagen cars. In the European Union this is set out in Article 14 of
Regulation (EU) 2017/1001; United States law calls it "nominative fair use".
To decide whether your use is of this kind, consider:

- whether you can identify the product or service without the trademark;
- whether you avoid a likelihood of confusion in the way you use it; and
- whether you used only as much as needed to identify the product or service.

The trainer above may say "Maude's training for Anima software". Using a
Logo is almost never necessary for this purpose.

## Proper trademark use

These rules apply to all trademarks, not just ours.

- Distinguish trademarks from the surrounding text with at least an initial
  capital: "Anima", not "anima".
- Use trademarks in their exact form, not abbreviated, hyphenated or combined
  with other words.
- Do not pluralize a trademark: "seventeen Anima applications", not
  "seventeen Animas".
- Use a trademark as an adjective: "an Anima application", not "an Anima".
- Do not use a trademark as a verb or a possessive.
- Do not translate a trademark.
- Do not change a Logo except to scale it, or to render it in black and
  white when the context requires it.
